# J815 capped speaker protection

`conf/apple/j815.conf` is the speaker-protection profile for the MacBook Air 15-inch (M5), J815. It drives all
six internal speakers at 48 kHz with the Aurora audio driver. Like the J813 profile, it targets comfortable
everyday playback rather than full macOS loudness. The amplifier ceilings are -24 dB for the four woofers and
-32 dB for the two tweeters, the values qualified on J813 (`docs/j813.md`).

The profile needs the J815 kernel audio support: six SN012776 codecs on i2c3, the AOP speaker back-end in its
six-amp layout, and the `apple,j815-macaudio` machine configuration with the kernel speaker interlock from
Linux #1000/#1048. The interlock keeps the amps at the safe level until this daemon holds the speaker lease and
relocks them when it goes away.

## Speakers and amplifiers

The six amplifiers are listed in the order of the ADT `audio-speaker` `speaker-config`: the primary amp
(0x38), then `speaker1` to `speaker5`. That is also the order of Apple's own speaker-protection channels.

| # | Speaker | I2C | V/I sense slots | Feedback channels (V, I) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Left Woofer 1 | 0x38 | 0 / 2 | 0, 1 |
| 1 | Right Woofer 1 | 0x3b | 4 / 6 | 2, 3 |
| 2 | Left Tweeter | 0x39 | 8 / 10 | 4, 5 |
| 3 | Right Tweeter | 0x3c | 12 / 14 | 6, 7 |
| 4 | Left Woofer 2 | 0x3a | 16 / 18 | 8, 9 |
| 5 | Right Woofer 2 | 0x3d | 20 / 22 | 10, 11 |

The section names must match each codec's `sound-name-prefix` in the device tree. The daemon finds each amp's
controls (`<name> VSENSE Switch` and so on) by these names.

## Where the values come from

- **Thermal and electrical constants: the 15-inch MacBook Air's, unchanged.** macOS ships one
  speaker-protection tuning for the J415, J615 and J815. The files are hard links to the same inode, so they
  are byte-identical. `docs/audump.py` prints exactly the values of `j615.conf` from both the J615 and J815
  tunings. The J815 therefore uses the same speakers and protection constants as `j615.conf`.
- **Limits: deliberately below Apple's.** As on J813, the model limit is 80 °C with 5 °C headroom (Apple:
  140 °C with 10 °C). The digital ceilings are -24 dB for woofers and -32 dB for tweeters. Lower limits only
  make the governor act earlier.
- **Feedback layout: measured.** The 12 S16 feedback channels are one voltage/current pair per speaker, in
  the order above, voltage first, as on J813. Left Woofer 1's voltage lags its current by one sample at
  48 kHz, also as on J813, so its section sets `current_delay_samples = 1` with `current_delay_rate = 48000`.

The profile contains no Apple DSP code or tuning bundle.

## Hardware qualification

All measurements were taken on one J815 (macOS 27 firmware, Aurora Linux) with this profile and the
unmodified daemon from `main`.

- **Probe and ceilings.** All six amps probed. Every `VSENSE`/`ISENSE` switch, `Amp Gain Volume` and
  `Speaker Volume` control was present. The feedback PCM opened with 12 channels at 48 kHz. With the daemon
  running, the controls read back `Speaker Volume` 153 (-24 dB) on all four woofers and 137 (-32 dB) on both
  tweeters.
- **Low-level electrical sweep.** The daemon was restarted with `-v --sense-capture`. One exponential sweep
  per speaker was played through it, one speaker at a time: woofers 80 Hz to 10 kHz at -12 dBFS, tweeters
  400 Hz to 16 kHz at -18 dBFS, 1.6 s each (`testing/make_j815_sweep.py`). That is about 2 mW per woofer
  and 0.2 mW per tweeter. `testing/verify_j815_sweep.py` passed every speaker:

  | Speaker | Pair | Crosstalk above noise | V/I order from | Lag (samples) | R_min (Ω) | z_nominal (Ω) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Left Woofer 1 | 0 | 5.7 % | envelope | voltage 1 late | 3.93 | 4.20 |
  | Right Woofer 1 | 1 | 5.1 % | envelope | 0 | 3.99 | 4.20 |
  | Left Tweeter | 2 | 0.0 % | phase | 0 | 3.37 | 3.76 |
  | Right Tweeter | 3 | 0.0 % | phase | 0 | 3.59 | 3.76 |
  | Left Woofer 2 | 4 | 5.6 % | envelope | 0 | 3.94 | 4.00 |
  | Right Woofer 2 | 5 | 5.2 % | envelope | 0 | 4.07 | 4.00 |

  Each speaker answered on its own pair; the other five pairs stayed at or below 5.7 % of it. With the profile's order and lag, each impedance is physical: a
  positive real part across the band, a resonance peak, and a rise toward high frequency from the voice-coil
  inductance. With the pair swapped or the lag changed, it is not. The impedance minimum is 0.90 to 1.02 of
  `z_nominal`. The daemon's per-period power lines matched an independent float64 replay of the capture
  within 7e-9 W.
- **Listening.** The sweep and ordinary playback at these ceilings were clear to the operator.

### How the sweep tools decide

- *Order:* during a sweep the amp's drive voltage is nearly flat while the current follows the impedance.
  For the woofers, the flatter channel of the pair is the voltage. The tweeters' drive rises over their
  sweep, so for them the order is the one that gives a physical impedance phase.
- *Lag:* a one-sample error at 48 kHz is about 75° of phase at 10 kHz. Only one lag leaves the
  high-frequency phase between 0 and +80°.
- *Crosstalk:* the other five pairs' level, measured above the capture's own noise floor during the lead-in
  silence.

## Not covered

This qualification covers the feedback layout and the capped ceilings. Higher ceilings, Apple's 140 °C
limit, other sample rates, and desktop routing and equalization are outside it. Each of those needs its own
qualification with sustained-playback thermal data. Keep the kernel locks, the profile limits and the
read-back checks throughout testing.
